# Mission System Design

## 1. Purpose of Missions
Missions are structured learning objectives that turn progression into meaningful achievement. They provide the bridge between individual lessons and the larger narrative arc of the experience. Missions should be durable, configurable, and tied to both learning value and emotional reward.

## 2. Mission Model
A mission should contain:
- a title and summary,
- objectives and acceptance criteria,
- prerequisites,
- reward definitions,
- difficulty and estimated duration,
- and completion conditions.

## 3. Mission Types
Possible mission types include:
- concept mastery missions,
- challenge streak missions,
- collaborative missions,
- exploration missions,
- and mastery review missions.

## 4. Mission Lifecycle
A mission should move through states such as:
- draft,
- active,
- in progress,
- completed,
- expired,
- and failed or abandoned.

## 5. Completion Rules
Completion should be based on clear and auditable conditions. Examples:
- solve a set of problems,
- reach a mastery threshold,
- finish a sequence of lessons,
- or collaborate with a squad to unlock a shared objective.

## 6. Reward Integration
Mission completion should feed rewards into the gamification and progression systems. Rewards may be:
- cosmetic progression unlocks,
- experience points,
- badges,
- or access to advanced content.

## 7. Dependency and Sequencing
Mission logic must be declarative so content authors can assemble sequences without changing application code.

## 8. Analytics and Feedback
Mission completion data should support reporting on engagement, retention, and difficulty balance.
